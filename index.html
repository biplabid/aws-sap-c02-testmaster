Overview

AWS SAP-C02 TestMaster is a practice platform for the AWS Certified Solutions Architect – Professional exam. It offers three ways to study — quick one-at-a-time questions, a short timed quiz, and a full-length mock exam — plus a statistics dashboard that tracks your progress across attempts.

The app runs entirely in your browser (or as a desktop app on Windows). Sign in with your Google account on the welcome screen to access it; your progress, answers, and attempt history are then saved locally on your device, namespaced to your signed-in account.

Getting Around

Use the top navigation bar (or the menu button on smaller screens) to switch between modes. The current section is always highlighted.

Dark Mode

Click the ◐ icon in the top-right corner to switch between light and dark themes. Your choice is remembered the next time you open the app.

Random Test

The most flexible way to study. Pick a question set and answer questions one at a time, in random order, with immediate feedback after each submission — correct/incorrect, the right answer, and the explanation.

  • Mark as Done — permanently excludes a question from future Random Test sessions for that set, so you can focus on questions you haven't mastered yet. This flag is remembered even after you close the app.
  • Reset Progress — clears all "done" flags for the currently selected set from the intro screen, if you want to start over.

Random Test has no timer and no scoring — it's meant for low-pressure, question-by-question learning.

Timed Quiz

A focused, 20-question quiz with a 40-minute countdown timer, meant to simulate a short section of the real exam.

  • Navigate freely between questions using Previous / Next or the question palette; your answers are saved automatically as you go.
  • The timer keeps running even if you switch away from the Timed Quiz view or close and reopen the app — it's based on elapsed real time, not just active screen time.
  • If time runs out, the quiz submits automatically with whatever answers you've selected so far.
  • Click Finish Quiz at any point to submit early.

Mock Exam

A full-length, 75-question simulation with a 3-hour timer — the closest thing to sitting the real SAP-C02 exam.

  • Question palette — jump to any question directly; answered questions and questions marked for review are color-coded.
  • Mark for Review — flag a question to revisit later without losing your place.
  • Review Screen — before submitting, see a full breakdown of answered, unanswered, and marked questions, with the remaining time.
  • Submit confirmation — you'll be warned if you have unanswered or marked questions before the exam locks in your answers.

Ask AI Coach

Ask AI Coach is available in two places, deliberately not during a timed/graded attempt itself:

  • Random Test — next to every question, since Random Test is untimed and has no scoring to protect.
  • Timed Quiz and Mock Exam results — only after you submit, on each question in the filtered Correct/Incorrect revision list (see Reading Your Results). It's not shown while the quiz or exam is still in progress.

Clicking it opens a panel docked to the right, below the header, automatically submits the question and its answer options, and shows the AI's explanation right there — no copying, no switching tabs. The rest of the page reflows to make room rather than being covered, so the question and header stay fully visible and usable. Click anywhere outside the panel (or press Esc) to close it.

First-time setup: a free Groq API key

Gemini Gems (the persona you configure at gemini.google.com) don't have a public API, so this panel instead calls Groq's free API, running the open-weight Llama 3.3 70B model with a system prompt written to match the AWS SAP-C02 Coach Gem's persona. The first time you click Ask AI Coach, you'll be asked to paste a Groq API key:

  1. Get a free key at console.groq.com/keys — no credit card required.
  2. Paste it into the panel and click Save Key & Ask.
ⓘ

The key is saved only in this browser's LocalStorage and sent only to Groq's API — never to any server of ours. Groq's free tier (14,400 requests/day, 30/minute) has no cost and no credit card requirement, and easily covers normal personal use. Change or replace the key any time from the Change AI coach key link at the bottom of the sidebar.

ⓘ

Llama 3.3 70B is a smaller, open-weight model rather than a proprietary flagship one — answers may occasionally be less precise or skip part of the requested structure (a table, an ASCII diagram) compared to a larger closed model. This is a deliberate cost/accuracy tradeoff, not a bug.

Reading Your Results

Both Timed Quiz and Mock Exam show the same kind of results screen after you submit: your score, how many questions you answered, and a per-question list.

Click the Correct or Incorrect summary tile to filter the question list down to just those questions. In this filtered view, each question expands to show:

  • The answer you selected (or "Not answered").
  • A Show correct answer & explanation button (for incorrect questions) that reveals the correct answer and the full explanation, so you can understand exactly why an answer was right or wrong.
  • An Ask AI Coach button (see Ask AI Coach) to dig deeper into any question, correct or incorrect.

Click the tile again, or the Show all questions button, to return to the full list.

Statistics

The Statistics dashboard aggregates every Timed Quiz and Mock Exam attempt you've completed:

  • Summary cards — total attempts, average score, best score, and total study time.
  • Score by Domain radar chart — shows your accuracy across different SAP-C02 exam domains, so you can see where to focus your remaining study time.
  • Recent Attempts bar chart — your last 10 attempt scores at a glance.
  • Attempt history table — every attempt with its date, type, score, and time used.
ⓘ

The dashboard only appears once you've completed at least one Timed Quiz or Mock Exam attempt.

Question Sets

Every practice mode has a Question Set selector. The app ships with several bundled sets covering different practice questions; any question sets you import via Upload appear in the same dropdown, in every mode.

Uploading Your Own Questions

The Upload page lets you add a new question bank from a specially formatted .docx file. Each question block must be separated by three dashes (---), and the file needs at least 20 questions. See the Upload page itself for the exact structure required.

⚠

Saving an uploaded set requires Chrome or Edge — it relies on the File System Access API to write the new set directly into the app's data folder. Other browsers can't save the file, though all other features work everywhere.

On success, you'll be asked to pick this project's data folder once; the new set is saved as the next available setN.json and immediately shows up as a selectable set everywhere.

Your Data & Privacy

TestMaster doesn't have a backend or a database of its own. Everything — your in-progress sessions, saved answers, timer state, "done" flags, attempt history, and theme preference — is stored locally in your browser's LocalStorage, namespaced to your signed-in Google account so it never leaves your browser.

This also means your local data is tied to a specific browser profile and device. Clearing your browser's site data for this app (or switching browsers/computers) will reset your progress and statistics.

Sign-in only identifies you — it requests the minimal openid email profile scopes and nothing else. You can revoke access at any time from your Google Account permissions page.

Refreshing the page doesn't sign you out

Reloading the page (or reopening the app) picks your session back up automatically — you won't be asked to sign in again, and you won't see a Google account-picker popup, just for refreshing. You stay signed in until you explicitly sign out or the 10-minute idle timeout kicks in (see below), whichever comes first.

Your history follows you back in

Your statistics and attempt history are tied to your Google account, not to a single sign-in session. Sign out (or get signed out automatically — see below) and sign back in with the same Google account on the same browser, and your Statistics page, attempt history, and Random Test "done" flags are exactly as you left them.

Signing out clears your in-progress session

Signing out — whether you click Sign Out yourself or get signed out automatically after being idle (see below) — clears any in-progress Timed Quiz or Mock Exam (including its timer and answers) along with your cached sign-in, so nothing is left behind for the next person to use this browser. This does not touch your Statistics, attempt history, "done" flags, or your saved AI Coach API key, which stay put and come right back the next time you sign in.

Automatic sign-out after 10 minutes idle

To protect your session, TestMaster signs you out automatically after 10 minutes with no activity (no clicks, key presses, scrolling, or mouse movement). You'll land back on the welcome screen with a message explaining why. If you were mid-way through a Timed Quiz or Mock Exam when this happens, that attempt is cleared (see above) rather than resumed — sign in again to start a fresh one.

Troubleshooting

My Timed Quiz or Mock Exam didn't save my progress

Make sure your browser allows LocalStorage for this site (private/incognito windows sometimes restrict or clear it) and that you're not clearing site data between sessions.

The Upload button says my browser isn't supported

Uploading requires the File System Access API, currently available only in Chromium-based browsers (Chrome, Edge). Try one of those.

I want to restart a set from scratch

In Random Test, use Reset Progress to clear "done" flags for a set. For Timed Quiz or Mock Exam, use Start New Quiz / Start New Mock Exam on the results screen, or just start the mode again — a fresh, in-progress session always replaces a completed one.

Design Principles

Overview

TestMaster is a vanilla HTML/CSS/JavaScript single-page application — no build step, no bundler, no framework, and no backend. Every js/*.js file attaches a small module to a shared window.TestMaster namespace, and index.html loads them all as plain <script> tags in dependency order.

The same static files run two ways:

  • In a browser — served by any static file server (e.g. python -m http.server), so fetch() can load the bundled question JSON without hitting CORS restrictions.
  • As a Windows desktop app — an Electron shell (electron/main.js) starts a local HTTP server over the project root and loads it into a native window, so the exact same code runs unmodified in both contexts.

There is still no server-side persistence: all state lives in the browser's LocalStorage by default. The one place the app writes outside LocalStorage is the Upload feature, which saves a new question set file via the File System Access API. Access to the app itself is gated behind Google Sign-In (see Google Sign-In & the Login Gate). One feature does call an external API with real payload data: Ask AI Coach sends the current question's text directly from the browser to Groq's API.

App Shell & Routing

index.html contains every view as a sibling <section class="view" data-view="..."> inside one .content container. ui.js owns navigation:

  • Any element with a data-nav attribute (top nav links, sidebar links, home page mode cards) is a navigation trigger.
  • Clicking one pushes a history entry for #<view> and toggles the matching [data-view] section's active class — there's no client-side router library, just hash matching.
  • Activating a view dispatches a testmaster:view-change CustomEvent on window, which feature modules listen for to refresh their set selector or resume an in-progress session.
  • Alt+letter keyboard shortcuts and the dark/light theme toggle (persisted to LocalStorage) are also owned by ui.js.

Module Reference

Application Shell

app.js

Entry point. Builds the shared appState object and calls each module's init*Shell function in order.

ui.js

Hash-based view routing, dark/light theme toggle, keyboard shortcuts, toast notifications.

utils.js

Tiny DOM query helpers (qs/qsa) and number formatting used throughout.

Core / Shared Modules

question-engine.js

Fetches a question set, normalizes any raw JSON shape into the canonical question object, and provides shuffle/random-selection helpers. Falls back to two built-in placeholder questions if a set fails to load or normalizes to zero valid questions.

view-helpers.js

Lists available question sets (bundled + uploaded), resolves a set key to its JSON path, renders a question into the shared answer-form markup, and grades single/multi-select answers.

file-sets.js

Discovers uploaded sets remembered in LocalStorage and writes new setN.json files into data/ via the File System Access API.

storage.js

The only module that touches window.localStorage directly. Provides typed get/set helpers plus higher-level session, timer, answer, and statistics APIs.

timer.js

A persisted, elapsed-time-based countdown timer factory used by both Timed Quiz and Mock Exam, so a timer survives a page reload or an app restart.

exam.js

A minimal shared session-state placeholder (appState.exam); each mode currently keeps its own richer state (appState.quiz, appState.mockExam, appState.random) rather than a unified shape.

Practice Mode Modules

random.js

Random Test: untimed, one question at a time, immediate feedback, and per-question "Mark as Done" tracking keyed by question set.

quiz.js

Timed Quiz: 20 questions, 40-minute timer, question palette, filterable Correct/Incorrect results with per-question answer reveal.

mock.js

Mock Exam: 75 questions randomly drawn from every bundled question bank combined, 3-hour timer, mark-for-review, a pre-submission review screen, and the same filterable results view as Timed Quiz. The combined pool is rebuilt on the fly (with a progress bar) each time a new attempt starts.

analytics.js

Statistics dashboard: renders summary cards and two Chart.js visualizations (domain radar, recent-attempts bar) from attempt history.

upload.js

Parses a .docx file (via Mammoth.js) into the canonical question shape and hands it to file-sets.js to persist.

ai-coach.js

Backs every "Ask AI Coach" button — live in random.js, and only in quiz.js/mock.js's post-submission result rows. Opens a right-side panel, submits the question to Groq's OpenAI-compatible chat completions API (open-weight llama-3.3-70b-versatile) with a system prompt recreating the shared Gem's persona, and renders the Markdown answer in-app. Manages the user-supplied API key in LocalStorage.

Auth Modules

config.js

Holds the public Google OAuth Client ID and the AI Coach Gem URL — both safe to commit, unlike a client secret.

auth.js

Google Identity Services sign-in/out, access-token lifecycle, and the testmaster:auth-change event other modules react to.

auth-gate.js

Full-screen welcome/sign-in overlay that hides the entire app shell until auth.js reports a signed-in user.

account-ui.js

The header sign-in control and account popover: renders identity and the sign-out action.

idle-logout.js

Tracks user activity (clicks, keys, scroll, mouse movement) and calls auth.signOut() after 10 minutes with none, while a user is signed in.

Question Data Pipeline

Every question set on disk (data/set1.json … data/set5.json, plus anything saved via Upload) is a flat JSON array of raw question objects. Loading always goes through the same normalization contract in question-engine.js, regardless of the source file's exact field names:

Raw field (accepted aliases)Canonical field
question / prompt / textprompt
type (anything starting with "multi" → multi-select)type: "single" | "multiple"
options[] (string or {id/letter, text})options[]: {id, text}
answer / answers / correctAnswerscorrectAnswers[] (upper-cased letters)
domain, difficulty, explanationpassed through as-is (defaulted if missing)

Any raw question missing a prompt, options, or a correct answer is dropped during normalization. If an entire set normalizes to zero valid questions (or fails to fetch), question-engine.js silently falls back to two generic built-in questions so the UI never renders a blank state — see Known Limitations for why this matters.

Set selection flows through view-helpers.js: getAvailableQuestionSets() returns the bundled sets (discovered on the fly by probing data/set1.json, data/set2.json, ... until one 404s) plus any uploaded sets from file-sets.js, populateSetSelector() fills Random Test's and Timed Quiz's <select>, and resolveQuestionSetSource() turns a chosen key into a data/<key>.json path for question-engine.js to fetch. Mock Exam doesn't use a selector at all — it calls getAvailableQuestionSets() to fetch and combine every bank into one pool before each attempt (see below).

Practice Mode State

Timed Quiz and Mock Exam share the same lifecycle shape, each owning its own slice of appState (appState.quiz / appState.mockExam):

  1. Start — Timed Quiz shuffles and slices the selected bank to 20 questions immediately. Mock Exam instead fetches and combines every bundled bank on the fly, showing a progress bar as each one loads, then waits for the user to confirm "Start Test" once the combined 75-question set is ready. Either mode restores an in-progress session from storage.loadExamSession() instead, if one exists.
  2. In progress — every answer change and navigation saves the current answers and session pointer to LocalStorage immediately, and a persisted timer.js instance keeps counting down based on elapsed wall-clock time.
  3. Finish — triggered manually or by timer expiry; computes the score, records the attempt via storage.recordAttempt(), clears the session, and renders the filterable results view.

Random Test (random.js) is simpler and stateless across reloads: it holds an in-memory shuffled pool for the current visit and persists only the per-question "done" flags, keyed by question set.

LocalStorage Schema

Every key storage.js writes is namespaced under sap-c02-testmaster: and JSON-encoded:

KeyShapePurposeScope
settings{ theme, updatedAt }Theme preferenceShared
statisticsAggregate countersAttempts, average/best score, study timePer user
attempt_historyArray of attempt recordsBacks the Statistics page (list, charts)Per user
session:<id>{ status, currentIndex, questions, sessionOwner, ... }Resumable in-progress quiz/mock sessionShared
timer:<id>Timer state snapshotCountdown survives reloadsShared
answers:<id>{ [questionId]: string[] }Selected answers for a sessionShared
review:<id>{ [questionId]: true }Mock Exam "marked for review" flagsShared
done_questions:<setKey>Array of question IDsRandom Test "Mark as Done" per setPer user
ai-coach:groq-api-keyStringUser-supplied Groq API key for Ask AI CoachShared

<id> is the session id ("timed-quiz" or "mock-exam"). storage.clearExamSession() clears the session, answers, review flags, and timer together once an attempt finishes. storage.clearActiveSessions() scans for and clears every session:* key this way in one pass; auth.js calls it on explicit sign-out (manual or idle-timeout) so an in-progress attempt never lingers for the next person to sign into this browser — it deliberately leaves per-user statistics/attempt_history/done_questions untouched.

Per-user scope means the key is additionally prefixed with the signed-in user's stable Google id (sap-c02-testmaster:<sub>:<key>) via storage.setCurrentUser(), which storage.js itself calls in response to testmaster:auth-change — so two people signed into different Google accounts on the same browser never see each other's history or done-flags. Shared keys (in-progress session/timer/answer/review state) are deliberately left un-namespaced: those modules read from storage synchronously at bootstrap, before Google auth can possibly have resolved (it's always asynchronous), so a signed-in user's very first render would otherwise happen under the wrong prefix. random.js and analytics.js additionally listen for testmaster:user-change to refresh their per-user-scoped displays (done-count, statistics) if sign-in resolves or changes while already on that view.

Upload Pipeline

Custom question banks flow through three modules in sequence:

  1. upload.js reads the selected .docx as an array buffer, extracts raw text with Mammoth.js, splits it into ----delimited blocks (minimum 50), and parses each block's Question / Type / Domain / lettered options / Answer / Explanation lines into the canonical question shape.
  2. file-sets.js requests write access to the project's data folder via window.showDirectoryPicker(), determines the next free setN.json name, and writes the parsed questions to it.
  3. The new file name is remembered in LocalStorage (file_sets_index) so view-helpers.js can list it as a selectable set in every mode on subsequent visits, without needing to re-scan the file system.
⚠

Both the directory picker and the write itself depend on the File System Access API, which only Chromium-based browsers implement — there is no fallback download-a-file path for other browsers.

Google Sign-In & the Login Gate

Sign-in is mandatory: the app shell (.app-shell, everything in the header and the practice-mode views) stays hidden behind a full-screen welcome overlay until auth.js reports a signed-in user, via Google Identity Services' client-side token flow (the old gapi.auth2 library was sunset in 2023). There is no backend, so this is an authorization flow (an access token), not a verified ID-token/JWT identity flow — acceptable because the gate only ever needs to know "is someone signed in," never a server-verified identity.

Sign-in

auth.js calls google.accounts.oauth2.initTokenClient() requesting the minimal openid email profile scopes, then calls Google's userinfo endpoint with the resulting token to get { sub, email, name, picture }. It dispatches testmaster:auth-change on every sign-in/out, which storage.js, auth-gate.js, and account-ui.js all listen for independently.

The gate

auth-gate.js renders the branded welcome screen (#authGate in index.html) with a "Sign in with Google" button and toggles two CSS classes based on the current user: the gate is shown and .app-shell stays hidden while signed out; once testmaster:auth-change reports a user, the gate hides and the app shell (defaulting to the Home view) becomes visible. No view, route, or feature module needs to know about the gate itself — it sits entirely outside .app-shell and only ever toggles that one element's visibility.

A page reload trusts the cached profile outright rather than re-verifying it with Google: the gate's very first render (before auth.js's init() has even finished waiting for Google Identity Services to load) uses auth.getCachedUser() — a synchronous read of the last-known profile from LocalStorage — to unlock the app immediately. init() itself does the same once it resolves, and deliberately does not follow up with a silent requestAccessToken({ prompt: "" }) call the way it originally did — that call is meant to be invisible, but Google can still surface its own account-chooser popup for it, which defeated the entire point. Since nothing in the app calls a Google API with the access token (Drive sync is gone), there was nothing that call was actually protecting. The session now ends only via an explicit sign-out (manual, or idle-logout.js's 10-minute timeout) — see Known Limitations for the tradeoff this makes.

Analytics Pipeline

analytics.js reads attempt_history and statistics from storage whenever the Statistics view activates. Per-domain accuracy for the radar chart is computed by re-grading every stored attempt's saved questions/answers against viewHelpers.isAnswerCorrect() and grouping by each question's domain field. Both charts are rendered with Chart.js and re-themed (grid/label colors) on every render based on the current light/dark theme.

Desktop Packaging

electron/main.js is the entire Electron main process: on app.whenReady() it starts a local HTTP server (serve-handler) bound to an OS-assigned port over the project root, then opens a BrowserWindow pointed at http://127.0.0.1:<port>/index.html. Context isolation and Node integration are disabled in the renderer (contextIsolation: true, nodeIntegration: false, sandbox: true) — the desktop app has no elevated capabilities beyond what the same page could do in a regular browser tab.

package.json's build field configures electron-builder to produce two Windows targets from npm run dist:

  • NSIS installer — per-user, no admin rights, with Desktop and Start Menu shortcuts.
  • Portable executable — runs directly with no installation step.

Both bundle index.html, css/, js/, data/, and assets/ as-is (via asar packaging) — there's no separate desktop build artifact to keep in sync with the web version.

Known Limitations

⚠

Mock Exam attempts are missing from domain analytics. mock.js's call to storage.recordAttempt() omits the questions/answers fields that quiz.js includes. analytics.js's domain radar chart re-grades attempts from those fields, so Mock Exam attempts silently contribute nothing to the per-domain breakdown even though they still count toward the summary cards and history table.

⚠

data/set4.json has no usable questions. Every entry in that file has an empty options and answer array. question-engine.js normalizes it down to zero valid questions and silently falls back to the two generic built-in questions, repeated to fill the requested count.

⚠

Upload is Chromium-only, with no fallback (e.g. a plain file download) for Firefox or Safari users who want to import a custom set.

⚠

No refresh token, so the app trusts a cached profile indefinitely rather than truly persisting a verified sign-in. Google's client-side token flow never issues a refresh token (those only exist in the server-side auth-code flow, which needs a backend this app doesn't have). Rather than re-verifying with Google in the background on every load — which risked surfacing Google's own account-chooser popup and defeated the point of a refresh-safe session — auth.js simply trusts the cached profile in LocalStorage until an explicit sign-out (manual, or idle-logout.js's 10-minute timeout). The access token itself lives in memory only and is set solely by an explicit signIn()/signOut() action; nothing re-derives it on a page load. Practical effect: if someone's Google access is revoked externally (account deleted, app access revoked from their Google account settings) mid-session, TestMaster won't notice until they next explicitly sign in or out — acceptable since nothing in the app calls a Google API with that token (Drive sync is gone), so a stale cached identity has no real access to lose.

⚠

Google Sign-In does not work in the packaged Electron desktop app, so the desktop build is currently unusable. Google blocks OAuth inside embedded/webview browsers by design (an anti-phishing measure), and Electron's BrowserWindow is exactly that. Since sign-in is now a hard gate in front of the entire app, the desktop .exe can no longer get past the welcome screen — this is a known, accepted tradeoff for now, not a bug.

⚠

Ask AI Coach's system prompt is a re-implementation, not the literal Gem, running on a different (smaller, open-weight) model. Gemini Gems have no public API, so config.js's AI_COACH_SYSTEM_PROMPT approximates the shared Gem's persona rather than calling it directly, and it's answered by Groq's free llama-3.3-70b-versatile rather than Gemini. Answers may drift from what the Gem itself would say, and may less consistently include every requested section (table, ASCII diagram) than a larger model would — an accepted cost/accuracy tradeoff. Keep the prompt in sync manually if the Gem's instructions change.

⚠

The Groq API key is visible to whoever holds it, by nature of a backend-less app. It's stored in LocalStorage and sent directly from the browser to Groq, never through a server of ours — but that also means it's visible in that browser's dev tools/network tab like any client-side credential. Groq's free tier has no billing attached to a leaked key, which limits the downside compared to a paid API key.

⚠

No automated test suite. There's no unit, integration, or end-to-end test tooling configured in package.json; correctness currently relies on manual verification.

Extending the App

Adding a new practice mode

Create a new js/<mode>.js module following the existing pattern (an IIFE attaching to window.TestMaster.<mode>, an init<Mode>Shell(appState) entry point), add its markup as a new [data-view] section in index.html, register it in app.js's bootstrap sequence, and add matching [data-nav] links in the header and sidebar.

Adding a new question set

Either drop a new data/setN.json file (array of raw questions matching the schema in Question Data Pipeline) and add it to the BUILT_IN_SETS list in view-helpers.js, or let a user add one at runtime through the Upload page.

AWS SAP-C02 Coach

Paste a free Groq API key to enable AI Coach answers in this browser. Get one at console.groq.com/keys (no credit card needed) — it's saved only in this browser's storage, never sent anywhere but Groq's API.
